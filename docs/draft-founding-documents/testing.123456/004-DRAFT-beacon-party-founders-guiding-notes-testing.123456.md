## TL;DR

- **Purpose:** be the party that steers Britain through the AI transformation fairly, safely and honestly, and is trusted to govern everything else while doing it.
- **Start by listening:** before deciding anything, hold 100+ real conversations to understand what people think, feel, worry about and hope for. Build the party around that, not around marketing.
- **Edge:** honesty. In a country where only 9% trust politicians, the **Honest Beacons Charter** (public tracking, no quiet U-turns) is the party's most distinctive asset. Protect it above everything.
- **Strategy:**
  - Win the argument first, by forcing the big parties to adopt Beacon ideas, as UKIP did.
  - Build credibility through experienced recruits and sound governance.
  - Target proportional elections (Holyrood, the Senedd), councils and by-elections before Westminster.
- **Biggest risks:**
  - a personality-driven split (Your Party);
  - unclear purpose (Change UK);
  - capture by AI-industry money;
  - being seen as a "tech party";
  - dying of success if the big parties steal the ideas (UKIP). The defence is that **the AI transition never "finishes"**, so the party's job doesn't either.

## 1. Hold on to these, whatever happens

These are the Core Founding Principles and the commitments that flow from them. They should be written into the party constitution and made hard to change.

1. **Main focus:** AI, and how best to handle its opportunities and challenges in the UK over the coming years and decades.
2. **The three Assumptions:**
   - AI is already major and growing fast.
   - It will create a bumpy road for jobs, society and safety.
   - If navigated well, it can lead to shared abundance for everyone, every nation and the planet.
3. **The Beacons:** always guided by named, dated Beacons with concrete steps for Now, the Short Term and to the Beacon Year. They will change as the world changes, and every change is explained to voters in real time.
4. **The Honest Beacons Charter:** no quiet U-turns; public tracking; independent scorekeeping; abandonment only by public statement and a member vote.
5. **Not a tech party:** we don't exist to accelerate AI or to stop it, but to manage the transition.
6. **Go first where it's safe, coordinate where it isn't:** Britain leads by example on domestic measures. Where acting alone would drive investment away, it builds international agreement (the Global Treaty Beacon).
7. **Fairness with reward:** a rising floor for everyone, and a ladder that still rewards effort and talent.

## 2. Listen first: understand people before deciding anything

**This comes first, in time as well as on the page.** Before positioning, slogans, policy detail or a launch date, the founders should spend months **listening**: finding out what people across the UK actually think, feel, worry about and hope for as AI changes their lives.

**This is not a marketing technique.** The point is not to find the words that "sell" a party already decided. It is to **understand people well enough to build the party around their real lives**: which problems matter most, which Beacons are urgent, which fears are real, and what a better future would mean to them. If we listen properly, some of our assumptions will turn out to be wrong. That is the point.

### Why ordinary questions mislead

Rob Fitzpatrick's *The Mom Test* (written for start-ups) makes a simple observation that applies to politics too:
- If you ask people what they think of your idea, they will usually be polite, and tell you something that isn't true.
- Questions about the future ("Would you…?") get guesses.
- Questions about the past ("When did you last…?") get facts.

Much political research works on hypotheticals ("Would you support…?", "Would you vote for…?"). The research document notes that this makes much of the polling on AI weaker evidence than it first appears.

### Rules for listening

1. **Talk about their life, not about the Beacon Party.** Don't mention or pitch the party or the Beacons in listening conversations. Ask about work, family, money, their town and their hopes.
2. **Ask about specific things that have already happened, not opinions about the future.**
3. **Listen far more than you talk.** If you are explaining, you are not learning.
4. **Notice what people have already done.** Have they started retraining, cut back spending, taken on a second job, or raised it with a union? Real worries show up in actions.
5. **Treat compliments with care.** "Sounds great, I'd vote for that" tells you little. Real interest shows in **commitment**: joining the mailing list, giving an evening, introducing a friend, coming back to the next meeting.
6. **Look for hopes as well as fears.** Ask what a good future would look like for them and their children. The Beacons are meant to describe destinations people actually want to reach.

### Example questions

| ❌ Avoid | ✅ Ask instead |
|---|---|
| "Are you worried about AI taking jobs?" | "When did AI, or new technology, last change something at your work? What happened next?" |
| "Would you support a Transition Income?" | "If someone you know has lost a job recently, how did they manage for money in the first few months?" |
| "Would you vote for an AI-focused party?" | "When did politics last say anything useful about your job or your kids' prospects?" |
| "Do you trust politicians?" | "Tell me about the last political promise you remember. What happened to it?" |
| "Is AI good or bad?" | "What's one thing AI has made better or worse for you or your family this year?" |
| "What should the government do?" | "What did you wish someone had done the last time things got hard for you financially?" |

### How to do it

- **Aim for 100+ conversations before launch.** Hold them in workplaces, pubs, cafés, school gates, community centres and online communities.
- **Who to talk to:** reach the people the party hopes to serve (mid-career office workers, parents, young people shut out of entry-level jobs, people in towns that lost industries before). Also include **sceptics, people who aren't worried about AI at all, and supporters of every other party**.
- **Record and share:** short, anonymised notes in a shared log, covering what happened, what people did, what they feel, and what they hope for. Ideally have two people at each conversation.
- **Review together every month:** look for patterns, surprises, and anything that contradicts our assumptions.
- **Let it shape the party.** What we hear should change:
  - which Beacons come first;
  - their dates and details;
  - the words we use;
  - whether the honesty pitch even lands.
  
  The Core Founding Principles are fixed by this project. But if listening suggests even one of them is wrong, say so openly. That's the Honest Beacons approach applied to ourselves.
- **Keep listening after launch.** Listening never finishes. Make it a standing part of how the party works, alongside the Annual Beacon Review.
- **Ethics:** tell people who you are and why you're asking; get consent; record nothing without permission; collect as little personal data as possible; follow UK GDPR.
- **Use alongside polling, not instead of it.** Conversations tell you *why*, and polls tell you *how many*. Treat hypothetical poll questions with care.

## 3. Positioning

**One-line pitch:**

> *"AI is changing everything. We're the party with a plan to get Britain through it, fairly, safely and honestly."*

**Who it's for:** people who over the next year become steadily more worried about AI and don't see the government doing enough, especially on jobs. Polling suggests this is already the majority:
- 7 in 10 are worried about AI's economic impact (KCL, 2026);
- 80% lack confidence in governments to regulate AI (YouGov, 2026).

**Core early audiences:**
- Mid-career white-collar workers (admin, finance, legal, IT, marketing, customer service) who can see AI changing their own jobs.
- Parents worried about their children's job prospects.
- Young people shut out of entry-level work.
- Tech workers who build AI and want it to benefit everyone. In the US they formed the Guardrails Alliance.
- People who have given up on the main parties but don't want populist anger.

**Tone:**
- Calm, honest and evidence-led. Never apocalyptic, never dismissive.
- Say "we might be wrong about the timing, and here's how we'll adapt" instead of making overconfident predictions.
- Always link AI to everyday life: jobs, bills, homes, the NHS, kids' futures.

**Avoid:**
- Sounding like a tech company (jargon, hype, "disruption").
- Sounding like a doom movement.
- Promising specific numbers before independent costing.

## 4. Lessons from parties that came before

| Party | What happened | Lesson for us |
|---|---|---|
| **UKIP** | Dismissed as single-issue for years. Grew by linking the EU to an everyday concern (immigration) and pressuring the Conservatives into a referendum, then collapsed after winning: "Brexit marked UKIP's high point and moment of triumph, yet it also sealed the party's doom." ([Australian Outlook](https://www.internationalaffairs.org.au/australianoutlook/life-death-ukip/)) | Link AI to everyday life. Pressuring the big parties is a valid route to success. **Plan for life after the big parties copy us.** The AI transition is ongoing, so our role is too |
| **Brexit Party → Reform UK** | Won the 2019 European elections but no Westminster seats. Renamed and broadened in 2020; later attracted many experienced Conservative defectors once it was polling well | Broaden beyond the core issue early. Momentum attracts talent |
| **SDP (1981)** | Big-name defectors and strong polling, but first-past-the-post gave it only a handful of seats. Merged with the Liberals | Under first-past-the-post, evenly spread support wins little. Concentrate effort geographically and target proportional systems. Be open to alliances |
| **Change UK (2019)** | 11 MPs, unclear purpose, collapsed within months: "the eleven MPs are less recognisable than the Social Democrats' leaders" ([University of Birmingham](https://www.birmingham.ac.uk/research/perspective/lessons-to-learn-from-the-sdp-for-the-independents)) | A clear purpose matters more than defectors. Don't launch before the purpose and the plan are ready |
| **Your Party (2025–26)** | 55,000 members early on, then leadership battles, rival slates and breakaways within a year ([QMUL](https://www.qmul.ac.uk/mei/news-and-opinion/items/long-read-a-year-of-your-party.html)) | **Governance before growth.** Agree leadership rules, decision-making and dispute resolution before going public |
| **Team Mirai (Japan, 2025–)** | Founded by an AI engineer; 11 lower-house seats within a year under proportional representation | AI-era parties can break through fast, especially under PR. But Team Mirai is pro-tech, and we are pro-*transition* |

## 5. A phased founding plan

Dates are indicative, and they are Beacons for the party itself: track them publicly too.

### Phase 0: The conversation (now to mid-2027)
- **Listen first (section 2):** 100+ listening conversations with people across the UK. Review them monthly, and let them shape the Beacons, priorities and language before anything is launched.
- Collect and publish Draft Founding Documents through the GitHub repo. Run discussions on Discord.
- Synthesise the drafts into **one consolidated set**: principles, Beacons, constitution.
- Build an email list and community. Run small public events. Find a **founding team of 5–10 people** with complementary skills: policy, law, finance and compliance, communications, organising, and at least one person with real political or government experience.
- Contact allied organisations (section 9).
- **Use the UK's G20 presidency (December 2026 to November 2027).** The PM is proposing a "Manchester Compact on AI" for the Manchester summit in November 2027. Campaign publicly for it to include **economic** terms: a minimum AI tax, worker protection, and sharing the gains. It's an early, high-profile chance to show what the Global Treaty Beacon means. The message: "A compact is a promise. We want a treaty."

### Phase 1: Founding (mid-2027 to end-2027)
- Agree the **constitution**, including the Honest Beacons Charter, leadership rules and dispute resolution.
- Appoint a **registered leader, nominating officer and treasurer**, and register with the **Electoral Commission**. Older guidance listed a £150 fee, an application, a constitution and a financial scheme; **check the current requirements**.
- Set up compliant finance: a bank account, a donations policy, and permissibility checks.
- Launch publicly with the **public Beacon tracker live on day one**.

### Phase 2: First tests (2028 to 2029)
- Stand in **council elections and by-elections** in a small number of target areas with high AI exposure, such as back-office and service centres, and university towns with graduate-jobs worries.
- Run a high-profile **campaign on Bridge**. It is the most urgent and relatable Beacon.
- **General election (due by August 2029):** stand selectively where we can make an impact, and use the campaign to push Beacon ideas into the national debate. Consider non-aggression arrangements where it helps get the ideas adopted.

### Phase 3: Building strength (2029 to 2031)
- Target the **Senedd (2030)** and the **Scottish Parliament (2031)**, where proportional systems give a new party a realistic route to seats.
- Grow the bench of experienced people ready to run departments.

### Phase 4: Government or decisive influence (2030s)
- Whether through seats, coalition or the big parties adopting our Beacons, the goal is for the Beacons to be delivered.

## 6. Running the Beacons

The Beacons are the party's core product. Run them like a well-managed programme, in public.

- **Beacon Council:** a standing body of elected members, policy experts and an independent chair. It owns the Beacon set, proposes changes and publishes quarterly status reports.
- **Beacon owner:** each Beacon has one named spokesperson who is accountable for it.
- **Standard Beacon template:**
  - name;
  - Beacon Year;
  - destination (measurable);
  - why (evidence);
  - steps for Now, Short Term and to the Beacon Year;
  - metrics;
  - "what would make us change it";
  - owner;
  - version and changelog.
- **Public tracker:** a simple website (or this GitHub repo) showing each Beacon's status and full version history.
- **Change protocol:**
  1. A trigger: new evidence, events, or a member proposal.
  2. A Beacon Council review, with a written rationale.
  3. A public consultation period, except in emergencies.
  4. A published decision explaining what changed, why, and what happens to anyone affected.
  5. A changelog entry.
  6. For **abandonment**, a member vote.
- **Annual Beacon Review:** a set-piece public event each year to update the forecasts and the Beacons. Make transparency a moment voters look forward to.
- **Invite independent scoring:** ask Full Fact or academics to rate the party's own Beacons. Its Government Tracker shows how vague pledges become unrateable, so make ours precise.

## 7. Governance: avoid the split

New parties most often die from internal conflict, not opposition.

- **Leadership:** a single elected leader with a fixed term and a clear recall mechanism. Avoid informal co-leadership; Your Party's experience shows the risk.
- **Decision-making:** written rules for who decides policy (members via conference and the Beacon Council), strategy (an executive) and day-to-day operations (staff).
- **Dispute resolution:** an independent disputes panel and a code of conduct, agreed **before** launch.
- **Founder humility:** no one person "owns" the party. The repo owner and early founders should commit publicly to stepping back if members choose others.
- **Openness:** publish governance documents, minutes of key decisions and finances.
- **Local branches:** a national registration covers branches. Give them a clear remit and support, but keep the Beacons nationally owned for consistency.

## 8. People: recruiting those who can govern

The human's insight: **absorb people from the main parties who want a more radically AI-focused party, but who can also do the jobs of running the "normal" things well.**

- **Target recruits:**
  - Former ministers, MPs, councillors and civil servants, from all parties.
  - NHS managers and clinicians, teachers and headteachers, police and military officers.
  - Economists, business leaders and trade unionists.
  - AI researchers and engineers who care about fair outcomes.
- **The offer:** "you've seen this coming and your party isn't treating it seriously. Come and help write the plan, and run the department you know."
- **Shadow portfolios:** appoint experienced spokespeople for every major department early, so the party is visibly ready to govern beyond AI.
- **Talent before defectors:** Reform shows that defectors come once a party has momentum. Build credibility first, then recruit high-profile names. Vet all recruits for alignment with the Charter.
- **Diversity:** recruit from every nation and region, and across ages and backgrounds. AI's impact is broad, and the party should be too.

## 10. Money, independence and compliance

- **Comply fully** with the Political Parties, Elections and Referendums Act 2000: accept donations only from permissible sources, report as required, and keep within spending limits. Appoint a competent treasurer from day one.
- **Independence from the AI industry:**
  - Cap any single donor's share of income.
  - Publish **all** donations, not just those above the legal thresholds.
  - Refuse donations with policy conditions.
  - Be especially careful with AI companies and their executives. Being captured by the industry would destroy the party's credibility.
- **A small-donor model:** follow the example of the US Guardrails Alliance, which is funded by modest contributions from people inside the industry. Membership fees and small recurring donations build independence.

## 11. How the party uses AI itself

A party about AI will be judged by how it uses AI.

- **Openness:** label all AI-generated or AI-assisted content (including these drafts, which are AI-assisted).
- **No deepfakes, ever.** Not of opponents and not of ourselves. AI personas have already appeared in UK election campaigns, and the Electoral Commission is now monitoring AI content.
- **Use AI well:** for policy research, translating documents, accessibility, and helping members take part, as this project does. Keep humans accountable for every decision.
- **Data protection:** handle member and voter data to the highest standard.

## 12. Risks and how to handle them

| Risk | Why it's real | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Dying of success** (the big parties copy us) | It happened to UKIP | Count it as a win. The AI transition is ongoing and needs continuous Beacons. Our honesty model is hard for others to copy |
| **Leadership split** | Your Party, the SDP | Governance and dispute rules before launch. Fixed terms. Founder humility |
| **Unclear purpose** | Change UK | The Core Founding Principles and Beacons *are* the purpose. Keep pointing back to them |
| **Seen as a tech party or as doomsayers** | Team Mirai (pro-tech) and PauseAI (anti) occupy the ends | Consistent "politics of transition" message, always linked to everyday life |
| **Industry capture** | US pro-AI PACs spend $100m+ | Donation caps, full transparency, no conditional money |
| **Our timelines are wrong** | Forecasts vary widely (early 2030s to late 2040s) | The Beacons are built to change openly. The Annual Beacon Review recalibrates them |
| **First-past-the-post wipeout** | SDP, Brexit Party (2019) | Target proportional elections and concentrated areas. Treat influence on the agenda as success |
| **"Economic suicide" attack:** opponents say our taxes and rules will drive AI investment abroad | Stargate UK pause; US tariff threats over the Digital Services Tax | The **go first where safe, coordinate where not** rule. Domestic support and public equity happen now. Profit taxes and frontier licensing go through the Global Treaty. Explain this clearly and often |
| **Costings challenged** | All figures are currently illustrative | Independent costing (OBR/IFS) before any election. Publish assumptions |
| **Credibility on non-AI issues** | Single-issue reputation | Experienced shadow portfolios early. Mainstream, competent positions on other areas |

## 13. What success looks like

Success is not only seats. In rough order of achievability:

1. **The conversation changes:** "AI transition" becomes a mainstream political topic, and the big parties publish transition plans.
2. **Beacon ideas are adopted:** for example, a Transition Income, public equity in AI investment, or a citizens' AI fund becomes government policy, whoever is in power.
3. **Honest tracking spreads:** other parties start publishing measurable, tracked commitments.
4. **The party wins elected representatives:** councillors, then MSs/MSPs, then MPs.
5. **Government or decisive influence**, and the Beacons delivered.
