## TL;DR

- **The problem:** AI is already reducing hiring in the UK. The middle-income, white-collar workforce is next. The government is studying the problem but has no plan to support people through it. The value AI creates is flowing overseas. Almost nobody trusts politicians to tell them the truth about any of it.
- **Our answer:** a party whose central purpose is to **steer Britain through the AI transformation**. We do this with six dated, measurable **Beacons**:
  - **Bridge (2028):** a Transition Income for people displaced by AI.
  - **UK AI Stake (2030):** invest, with public equity, where Britain can win.
  - **Safe AI (2030):** binding rules for the most powerful AI.
  - **Global Treaty (2032):** an international agreement on AI tax, safety and sharing the gains, so Britain doesn't have to act alone.
  - **Shared Abundance Fund (2032):** a dividend for every adult.
  - **Universal High Income (2038):** a rising income floor as AI takes on more of the work.
- **Our promise:** the **Honest Beacons Charter**. Every Beacon is tracked publicly. When the facts change, we change the Beacon openly and explain why. No quiet U-turns.
- **Not a tech party:** we don't exist to speed AI up or to stop it. We exist to make the transition **fair, safe and shared**, and to govern everything else competently while we do it.

## 1. Why the Beacon Party exists

### The change has started

The jobs impact of AI is no longer hypothetical.

> "UK vacancies nearly halved since their 2022 peak"
> — [Bank Underground, Bank of England staff blog, 6 Aug 2026](https://bankunderground.co.uk/2026/08/06/canaries-in-the-column-ai-exposure-and-the-uks-hiring-slowdown/)

> "a one standard deviation increase in AI exposure was associated with a 3.9% reduction in posting volume"
> — [DSIT & AI Security Institute, 28 Jan 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/assessment-of-ai-capabilities-and-the-impact-on-the-uk-labour-market/assessment-of-ai-capabilities-and-the-impact-on-the-uk-labour-market)

> "UK firms recorded an 8% net job loss linked to AI over the past year"
> — [eWeek, reporting Morgan Stanley research, 28 Jan 2026](https://www.eweek.com/news/ai-job-losses-uk-morgan-stanley-report/)

> "A fifth of employers (22%) have already made roles redundant or reduced hiring because of AI"
> — [King's College London, 19 May 2026](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/news/one-in-five-britons-think-ai-will-create-civil-unrest-study-finds)

Official bodies are right to say the evidence doesn't yet *prove* that AI is behind the whole hiring slowdown. But the direction is clear, and the capability curve is steep. The UK's own AI Security Institute reports that frontier models have:

> "far surpassed PhD-level expertise in biology, with chemistry fast catching up."
> — [AI Security Institute, 18 Dec 2025](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/5-key-findings-from-our-first-frontier-ai-trends-report)

We believe AI and robotics could be doing most of today's work within **10 to 15 years**. That is within the range of serious forecasts: forecaster communities currently centre on the early 2030s for "AGI", while academic surveys are later. We may be wrong about the date. We are confident about the direction. A responsible country plans for it.

### The people most exposed are the people least protected

The first wave hits entry-level and administrative roles. Morgan Stanley's research also found **mid-career professionals** among the hardest hit. These are the middle-income earners who have mortgages, children and the least margin for a sudden drop in pay. Universal Credit was never designed to catch a project manager, paralegal or software tester whose profession is shrinking.

### The government is studying, not planning

In September 2026 the AI minister said Britain was preparing for "unprecedented" AI-driven job losses ([City AM](https://www.cityam.com/?p=2461239)). In the Budget a month later, the headline commitment for workers was:

> "The Treasury will commit £10m a year to studying the impact of AI on employment"
> — [City AM, 24 Oct 2026](https://www.cityam.com/?p=2471048)

Research matters. But a study is not a safety net.

### The gains are leaving the country

> "less than £1 in every £6 spent with its biggest technology suppliers goes to British firms"
> — [The Register, 11 Nov 2026](https://www.theregister.com/public-sector/2026/11/11/uks-buy-british-tech-ambitions-hit-reality-as-less-than-1-in-6-stays-at-home/5308814)

When work moves from UK employees, who pay income tax, to AI services billed from overseas, the UK loses wages *and* tax revenue. Labour's Darren Jones MP has warned this could create:

> "'cracks in the very foundations' of the UK economy"
> — [IBTimes UK, 21 Sep 2026](https://ibtimes.co.uk/darren-jones-warns-ai-could-leave-britain-without-enough-jobs-1820859)

### People can see it, and don't trust anyone to fix it

> "Seven in 10 of the UK public are worried about the economic impacts of AI"
> — [King's College London, 19 May 2026](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/news/one-in-five-britons-think-ai-will-create-civil-unrest-study-finds)

> "79% have little to no confidence in AI companies to develop the technology responsibly"
> — [YouGov, 18 Sep 2026](https://yougov.com/en-gb/articles/55587-britons-are-increasingly-worried-about-the-impact-of-ai)

> "just nine per cent saying they trust politicians to tell the truth"
> — [Ipsos Veracity Index 2025](https://www.ipsos.com/en-uk/ipsos-veracity-index-2025)

Just **7%** of the public think AI's benefits will be shared fairly (King's College London, 2026). **That is the gap the Beacon Party exists to fill.**

## 2. Our Core Founding Principles

**Our main focus.** The Beacon Party's main focus is AI, and how best to handle its opportunities and challenges in the UK over the coming years and decades.

**Our assumptions.**
1. AI is already a major factor in economics and society, and its influence will rapidly increase in the coming months and years.
2. The rapid changes related to AI will create a **"bumpy road"** for many people, as jobs, society and our safety are affected.
3. The benefits of AI are potentially huge. If we navigate this bumpy road well, we may be able to reach a period of **shared abundance** for humanity, in every nation, and for the planet as a whole.

**Our Beacons.** We are always guided by a set of **Beacons**, each with a **Name** and a **Beacon Year**. Each describes a future point the country is aiming for, with concrete steps for **Now**, the **Short Term**, and **between now and the Beacon Year**. We set them now, knowing they will need to change as the situation evolves, and some may even be abandoned. Any change will be explained to voters in real time, so they stay informed and on board.

## 3. Our values: fairness, effort and shared abundance

We believe in two things at once, and we don't think they conflict.

1. **Effort and talent should still be rewarded.** People who work hard, build businesses, learn and take risks should be able to do better. Markets and enterprise have lifted billions out of poverty, and we don't want to abolish them.
2. **Everyone should share in what AI creates.** AI is trained on humanity's collective knowledge and built on public research, public infrastructure and public data. When machines do the work, the wealth they create shouldn't flow to a handful of companies. Its benefits must reach every citizen, safely.

Our model is **a floor and a ladder**:
- **The floor:** a guaranteed income and essential services that rise as AI makes the country richer, so nobody falls through.
- **The ladder:** wages, enterprise, ownership and achievement, which keep rewarding people who climb it.

A dividend from a national AI fund is like an inheritance we all share. It's not a wage, and it doesn't stop anyone earning more. The largest study of unconditional cash, in the US, found people "worked about 1.3 fewer hours a week" ([Route Fifty](https://www.route-fifty.com/management/2024/07/results-biggest-study-guaranteed-income-programs-are-finally/398260/)). That is a small effect, and some of those people used the time for education.

**Fairness**, to us, means:
- Those hit first and hardest by AI get help first.
- Every region and nation of the UK shares in the gains, not just London and the South East.
- Today's young people aren't shut out of the career ladders their parents climbed.
- The rules apply equally to the biggest AI companies and to everyone else.

## 4. What we are, and what we are not

**We are an AI-focused party.** For the next 5 to 20 years AI will be the defining force in British life, as the industrial revolution was for our great-great-grandparents. It will touch the economy, jobs, the NHS, schools, defence, democracy and the environment. We put it at the centre because that is where it belongs.

**We are not a technology party.** We are not here to cheer AI on, and we are not here to stop it. The transformation is coming regardless, driven by global companies and global competition. Our job is the **politics of the transition**:
- Who is protected.
- Who shares the gains.
- How we stay safe.
- How we keep people informed and in control.

**We intend to govern.** Like UKIP before us, we may change national policy just by making the big parties take this issue seriously. If so, that is a success. But our aim is to be **trusted to run the country**. That means being as serious about the NHS, the public finances and defence as we are about AI (see section 8).

## 5. The Honest Beacons Charter

Most people have stopped believing political promises, with good reason. When Full Fact tried to track the current government's manifesto pledges, it found that many could not be rated at all because they were worded too vaguely to measure ([Full Fact](https://fullfact.org/government-tracker/frequently-asked-questions/)).

The Beacon Party will be different by design. **This Charter is a founding rule. It is not up for negotiation.**

1. **Every Beacon has:** a name, a Beacon Year, a measurable destination, named steps for Now, the Short Term and to the Beacon Year, and a named owner (a minister or spokesperson).
2. **Public tracker:** the status of every Beacon and step is published online and updated at least quarterly. The status is one of *on track*, *at risk*, *off track*, *changed* or *abandoned*.
3. **Independent scorekeeping:** in government, we will legislate for an independent **Beacon Office** to rate our progress, modelled on the Office for Budget Responsibility. In opposition, we will invite an independent fact-checker to do it.
4. **No quiet U-turns:** any change to a Beacon is announced openly, with:
   - what changed;
   - the evidence that caused it;
   - why the new course makes sense;
   - what happens to people who were relying on the old one.
5. **Changelog:** every version of every Beacon stays public forever, so anyone can see how our plan has evolved.
6. **Abandoning a Beacon** requires a public statement and a vote of the party's members.

## 6. Our Beacons

For each Beacon: the destination, why it matters, our concrete steps, how we'll measure it, and what would make us change it.

### Beacon 1: Bridge (Beacon Year 2028)

**Destination:** by 2028, **anyone in the UK who loses their job because of AI can keep their home and their dignity while they find their next role.** They get a Transition Income, funded retraining and protection for their mortgage or rent.

**Why:** hiring is already falling in AI-exposed jobs. Mid-career, middle-income workers face the biggest drop in living standards and have the most to lose: homes, families, pensions. The current safety net (Universal Credit) is a low floor, built for a different kind of unemployment. Lord Stockwood, the government's own investment minister, has said:

> "we're going to have to think really carefully about how we soft-land those industries that go away"
> — [Fortune, 1 Feb 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/02/01/elon-musk-optional-work-fantasy-universal-basic-income-uk-minister-jason-stockwood/)

We will do the soft landing.

**Now (first 100 days in government):**
- Legislate for an **AI Transition Income**: *illustratively* 70% of previous earnings, capped (for example at £2,500 a month), for up to 18 months, then tapering. No requirement to take the first job offered.
- Create **AI Displacement Notices**: large employers must disclose when roles are cut or not backfilled because of AI, and consult staff and unions. This builds on IPPR's call for a "legal duty requiring employers to disclose and consult workers and trade unions" ([IPPR, 29 May 2026](https://www.ippr.org/media-office/overhaul-worker-rights-to-prevent-ai-driven-inequality-says-ippr)).
- Fund an emergency **mortgage and rent protection scheme** for displaced workers.

**Short term (to 2027):**
- Launch a **Worker Transition Levy** on large firms that reduce headcount through AI, following IPPR's "worker support levy" proposal. It funds retraining and the Transition Income.
- Offer **Retraining Accounts**: a personal budget for each displaced worker, usable for accredited courses, apprenticeships or starting a business.
- Publish monthly **AI Jobs Statistics** from the ONS, so the country can see the impact in real time.

**To 2028:**
- Full national rollout. Evaluate against a control group and publish the results.
- Raise the Universal Credit floor and make its rules fit for an AI economy (savings rules, work search conditions).

**How we'll measure it:**
- Share of AI-displaced workers who keep their homes.
- Share back in work, or in training, within 18 months.
- Average income drop for displaced workers.

**What would make us change it:**
- If displacement is far larger than expected, we bring forward elements of the Shared Abundance Fund and Universal High Income.
- If it is far smaller, we scale the scheme down and redirect funds to the AI Stake.

**Cost (illustrative):** if 300,000 workers a year claimed an average of £20,000, the gross cost would be about **£6bn a year**. That is before savings on other benefits and before Levy income. It must be independently costed.

### Beacon 2: UK AI Stake (Beacon Year 2030)

**Destination:** by 2030, Britain **owns a real stake in the AI economy**:
- Public investment comes with public equity.
- At least **£1 in every £3** of public AI spending goes to UK firms (today it is less than £1 in £6).
- The UK is a world leader in the AI layers it is best at: safety and assurance, health, finance and the creative industries.

**Why:** we can't outspend the US on frontier models. But we can stop the value draining away, and own a share of what we help build. Today the Sovereign AI Unit has "up to £500 million" ([DSIT](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-opportunities-action-plan-one-year-on/ai-opportunities-action-plan-one-year-on)), and a £31bn Stargate UK project was paused over "the cost of energy, which has become the highest in Europe, and regulatory uncertainty" ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/openai-halts-gbp31-billion-stargate-uk-project-over-rising-energy-costs-and-regulatory-deadlock)).

**Now:**
- Make **"no public money without public equity"** the rule. All public AI investment, grants and sovereign compute deals must take equity, revenue share or golden-share protections, held by the Shared Abundance Fund.
- **Buy British by default** for public-sector AI where a UK option is competitive. Publish the UK share of AI spend every quarter.

**Short term (to 2028):**
- Introduce **AI Power Contracts**: long-term, low-cost clean electricity for data centres in AI Growth Zones, with the zones paying back through equity and local jobs.
- Scale the Sovereign AI Unit into a **UK AI Investment Fund**. The *illustrative* target is £20bn of public co-investment by 2030, focused on:
  - AI safety and assurance;
  - NHS and life-sciences AI;
  - fintech and regulatory AI;
  - creative-industry AI that pays creators fairly;
  - specialised compute.

**To 2030:**
- Make the UK the **world's assurance hub**: a home for testing, certifying and insuring AI systems. This builds on AISI.
- Have regional AI clusters in every nation of the UK, each with a published jobs and investment target.

**How we'll measure it:**
- UK share of public AI spend.
- Value of public AI equity held.
- Private investment attracted.
- UK AI sector revenue (£33.6bn in the government's 2025 study).

**What would make us change it:** if a sector we back fails to gain traction, we will say so and reallocate, not keep pouring money in to save face.

### Beacon 3: Safe AI (Beacon Year 2030)

**Destination:** by 2030, the most powerful AI systems in the UK operate under **binding, independently enforced safety rules**. Britain leads a working **international agreement** on frontier AI, and UK elections are protected from AI manipulation.

**Why:** the UK's own foresight work warns:

> "AI could cause serious, potentially even existential harms, without government intervention."
> — [GO-Science, *AI Scenarios 2030*, 15 Jun 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-scenarios-2030-helping-policymakers-plan-for-the-future-of-ai/ai-scenarios-2030-helping-policymakers-plan-for-the-future-of-ai)

61% of the public would support slowing AI development "but still being allowed to continue" ([YouGov, 2026](https://yougov.com/en-gb/articles/55587-britons-are-increasingly-worried-about-the-impact-of-ai)).

**Now:**
- Put the **AI Security Institute on a statutory footing**, with powers to require pre-deployment testing of frontier models.
- Introduce a **law against undisclosed AI deepfakes** of candidates, and mandatory AI-content labelling in election material.

**Short term (to 2028):**
- Pass a **Frontier AI Act** with licensing and incident reporting for the most capable systems, and clear liability.
- Join and champion international initiatives, instead of standing aside from them.

**To 2030:**
- Achieve an operational international agreement on frontier AI safety standards, through the Global Treaty (Beacon 4), with the UK as a founding member and host of its evaluation work.

**How we'll measure it:** statutory powers in force; number of frontier models tested before deployment; international agreement signatories; recorded election deepfake incidents.

**What would make us change it:** if evidence shows certain risks have been resolved or were overstated, we will relax the specific rules openly. We will never relax them quietly.

### Beacon 4: Global Treaty (Beacon Year 2032)

**Destination:** by 2032, the UK has helped found, and has joined, a **Global Treaty on AI** among a large bloc of major economies. It sets:
1. **a minimum tax or windfall levy on AI profits**, so no country can undercut the rest and companies can't play countries off against each other;
2. **shared safety standards** for frontier AI, including pre-deployment testing;
3. **a global dividend mechanism**, so poorer nations share in AI's wealth too.

**Why:** some of what Britain needs to do would be economic self-harm if done alone:
- A £31bn data-centre project was paused over UK energy costs and regulatory uncertainty.
- The UK's modest Digital Services Tax has already drawn US tariff threats and put a trade pact "on ice" ([The Register, 16 Dec 2025](https://www.theregister.com/2025/12/16/us_uk_trade_deal/)).
- Coordination is possible but fragile. The OECD global minimum tax was agreed by around 140 countries, then US multinationals were largely exempted in 2026.

Our Assumptions also promise shared abundance "in every nation, and for the planet as a whole". **Britain can't deliver that alone.**

**Our rule: go first where it's safe, coordinate where it isn't.**
- We will act on our own on Bridge, public equity in UK investment, buy-British procurement, AI safety testing and election protection, because these don't drive investment away.
- On AI profit taxes, frontier licensing and a global income floor, we will build a coalition first, so Britain leads without standing alone.

**Now:**
- **Shape the Manchester Compact.** The UK holds the G20 presidency in 2027, and the Prime Minister has proposed a "Manchester Compact on AI" for the November 2027 summit ([City AM, 17 Nov 2026](https://www.cityam.com/?p=2479080)). We will campaign for it to include **economic** terms (minimum AI tax, worker protection, sharing the gains), not just principles on safety and transparency. A compact is a set of shared, voluntary principles. **A treaty is binding.** We welcome the Manchester Compact as a first step, but our destination is a treaty.
- **Join the coalition that already exists.** Sign the 22-nation declaration led by Finland and Norway, which calls for AI to remain under "human direction, oversight and control" ([FAIR EDIH, 22 Sep 2026](https://www.fairedih.fi/en/2026/09/22/finland-and-norway-lead-22-nation-call-for-global-ai-oversight-body-as-us-and-china-stay-out/)). The UK stood aside from it.
- **Ratify the Council of Europe AI Convention,** which the UK signed in 2024.

**Short term (to 2029):**
- Build a **club of willing countries**: the EU, Canada, Japan, Australia, the Nordics, Commonwealth partners.
- Agree a common **AI minimum tax** and safety standards.
- Use **market-access conditions** to make joining attractive and staying out costly. The rule would be: to sell frontier AI services in the club, meet the standards and pay the levy. This is the model that made the Montreal Protocol on the ozone layer work.
- Apply domestic **top-up taxes** on AI profits earned in the UK, in line with the club, as the OECD framework already allows.

**To 2032:**
- A binding treaty in force, with an international body for testing and standards (Finland has suggested one modelled on the IAEA).
- A **Global AI Dividend** fund, building on the Windfall Trust proposal and the UN's proposed Global Fund on AI.

**How we'll measure it:**
- Number of countries in the Treaty, and their share of global GDP.
- Share of frontier AI providers operating under its standards.
- Revenue raised through coordinated levies.
- Size of the Global AI Dividend.

**What would make us change it:**
- If the US and China stay out, we won't wait for them. We will go ahead with a bloc large enough to set terms by market size, and say so openly.
- If a wider deal becomes possible sooner, we will bring the Beacon Year forward.

### Beacon 5: Shared Abundance Fund (Beacon Year 2032)

**Destination:** by 2032, a **National AI Dividend Fund** owned by every citizen pays an **annual dividend to every UK adult**. It is funded by the AI Stake equity, windfall levies on exceptional AI profits, and a share of AI-related tax.

**Why:** just 7% of people believe AI's benefits will be shared fairly. IPPR has called for:

> "Redistributing windfall gains from Sovereign AI investments, back to the public"
> — [IPPR, 16 Apr 2026](https://www.ippr.org/media-office/government-risks-techlash-unless-it-shows-who-benefits-from-ai-ippr-warns)

The Windfall Trust's global dividend proposal aims "to establish a universal human income floor" ([Future of Life Institute](https://futureoflife.org/project/the-windfall-trust/)). Norway and Alaska show that citizen-owned funds work.

**Now:** legislate to create the Fund as an independent body, with its rules locked in the way the Bank of England's independence is. It receives all equity from Beacon 2 from day one.

**Short term (to 2029):**
- Introduce an **AI Windfall Levy** on exceptional profits of the largest AI providers operating in the UK. We will set it **in step with Global Treaty partners** (Beacon 4), so it raises money without driving investment away.
- Seek voluntary **Windfall Clause** commitments from AI companies. Work with the Windfall Trust and international partners on a global framework.

**To 2032:**
- Pay the first annual citizen dividend. The *illustrative* target is for the Fund to reach £100bn+ in assets by 2032, with the dividend set by a published, rules-based formula.

**How we'll measure it:** fund size; annual dividend per adult; share of AI-sector value captured for the public.

**What would make us change it:** if returns are lower than expected, we will publish revised projections and adjust the date or size openly.

### Beacon 6: Universal High Income (Beacon Year 2038)

**Destination:** by 2038, if AI and robotics are doing most of the work, as we expect, **every adult in the UK receives a guaranteed income high enough for a comfortable life**. Our target is at least the real-terms equivalent of today's median full-time take-home pay. People remain free to earn, build and achieve more on top.

**Why:** this is the long-term destination of our Assumptions, the "shared abundance" at the end of the bumpy road. Calum Chace, who popularised the "Economic Singularity", considers about 2040 "a very reasonable possibility" for the point where machines can do most paid work. Ben Luong's *Discontinuity Thesis* argues AI severs the link between wages and mass prosperity. If they are right, a high guaranteed income is necessary for a working economy and democracy, not a luxury.

**Now:** commission an independent **Universal High Income Pathway** study, with costed routes from today's welfare system to a UHI.

**Short term (to 2030):** run large, rigorous UK pilots of guaranteed income, building on the Welsh care-leavers pilot and the OpenResearch study. Raise the income floor step by step through Beacons 1 and 5.

**To 2038:** merge the Transition Income, Universal Credit and the citizen dividend into a single, rising **Universal Income**. Raise it towards the UHI target as AI productivity and Fund returns allow.

**How we'll measure it:** level of the guaranteed income compared with median earnings; poverty rate; share of national income going to households.

**What would make us change it:**
- **Our timeline is a bet.** If AI progress is slower, we will push the Beacon Year back and say so.
- If it is faster, we will bring it forward.
- Either way, the change will be on the public tracker with our reasons.

## 7. Paying for it

We will be honest: the public finances are tight. The OBR forecasts public sector net debt at around **95–96% of GDP** through the late 2020s ([OBR](https://obr.uk/brief-guides/public-finances/)). We will not promise money we don't have.

Our approach:

1. **Own a share of what we fund.** Public equity in AI (Beacon 2) builds an asset, not just a cost.
2. **Tax AI value before the income-tax base erodes.** Most UK tax comes from work. As AI replaces work, that base shrinks. US analysis by RAND found corporate tax rates would need roughly to **double** to replace lost labour-tax revenue ([RAND](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA4980-1.html)). We will:
   - shift the tax system gradually from labour towards **AI-generated profits, capital and economic rent**;
   - strengthen the **digital services tax**;
   - introduce an **AI Windfall Levy** on exceptional AI profits;
   - consult on **taxing compute or AI usage** by large providers.
3. **Go first where it's safe, coordinate where it isn't.** Domestic spending, public equity and UK procurement need no permission from anyone. Taxes on globally mobile AI profits are set together with Global Treaty partners (Beacon 4), so Britain doesn't price itself out of the AI economy.
4. **Use levies for specific purposes.** The Worker Transition Levy funds Bridge, and the Windfall Levy funds the Shared Abundance Fund. Voters can see where every pound goes.
5. **Phase spending with evidence.** Bridge scales with actual displacement. UHI rises only as AI productivity and Fund returns rise.
6. **Get independent costing.** All Beacons will be submitted to the OBR (and, in government, the Beacon Office) for costing before any election.

## 8. Governing everything else

AI is our focus, but a government must run the whole country. We will recruit experienced people, including former ministers, civil servants, council leaders, clinicians, teachers, business leaders and economists, from all parties and none, to run every department well. Our approach to each area starts from sound, mainstream practice and asks: *how does AI change this?*

- **The economy and public finances:** sound money, an independent Bank of England and the OBR. Fiscal rules that account for the AI transition.
- **The NHS and care:** use AI to cut waiting lists and paperwork, with patient data protected and owned publicly. Redeploy, rather than cut, staff freed up by AI. Invest in care, a sector where human contact matters most.
- **Education:** teach every child to think, create and work alongside AI. Make lifelong learning a right. Rebuild entry-level routes into careers that AI has removed.
- **Housing and cost of living:** protect displaced workers' homes (Bridge). Use AI-driven productivity to lower the cost of essentials.
- **Defence and security:** a strong defence, and AI-ready cyber security. NATO commitments maintained. Work with allies on AI security.
- **Energy and environment:** cheap clean power is now both a climate policy *and* an AI policy. Expand renewables, nuclear and grid connections. Data centres must pay their way and use clean energy.
- **Democracy and rights:** protect elections from AI manipulation. Strong data rights. Give citizens a voice in how AI is used in public services.
- **The four nations and the regions:** AI investment and Bridge support spread across Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland and every English region.
- **Immigration, justice, transport and other areas:** governed pragmatically and on the evidence. We will publish full policies as the party develops, drawing on the expertise of the people who join us.

## 9. How we differ from the other parties

| | The others | The Beacon Party |
|---|---|---|
| **Labour (government)** | Pro-adoption, skills programmes, studying the impact, contingency planning | A **published, funded plan** to support displaced workers now |
| **Conservatives** | Warn against over-regulation | Safety rules *and* investment. Regulation that protects people without driving away innovation |
| **Reform UK** | Deregulation; AI as a way to shrink the state | AI as a way to **share prosperity**, not just cut costs |
| **Liberal Democrats** | Digital sovereignty, British tech contracts | Sovereignty **plus public equity**, a citizens' dividend and a transition plan |
| **Greens** | Tighter regulation; public ownership floated | Regulation for the most powerful AI, a mixed economy, and rewarding enterprise |
| **All of them** | Promises often too vague to measure (Full Fact) | **Honest Beacons:** dated, measured, publicly tracked, changed only in the open |

## 10. What we will never do

- **Make a promise and quietly drop it.**
- **Take money with strings from AI companies.** We will publish all donations and cap any single donor's share.
- **Use AI to deceive voters.** All AI-generated campaign material will be labelled, and we will never use deepfakes.
- **Pretend the future is certain.** We will tell you what we think is coming and how confident we are. When we're wrong, we'll say so.
- **Leave people to face the bumpy road alone.**
